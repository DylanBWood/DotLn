## Goal alignment and limits

This order serves the mission of making a second lane cost nothing to read
about and nothing to recover. The generated table, the byte-identical
receipt and the absent prose all support that. F2 leaves one recovery path
where the operator must rebuild the collision from the heading history. That
contradicts criterion 3's promise of exactly one complete record.

**NoOp** would accept an incomplete record because every existing check is
green; the executable reproduction rules that out. **Naive Interventionism**
would have the verifier patch the helper. Instead, the executor's reordering,
fixtures and all passing claims are preserved, and only the uncovered path
is named.

The eight traps:

- **Policy resistance and shifting the burden:** preparation and the
  integration recorder still disagree after a stub failure, and the operator
  absorbs the gap. VER-001's miss already shifted part of that burden onto a
  second repair cycle.
- **Drift to low performance and rule beating:** green suite counts do not
  replace the required provenance.
- **Escalation:** none; the existing repair route suffices.
- **Tragedy of the commons:** no subagents; one full gate was reused at an
  unchanged code identity.
- **Success to the successful:** the recorded publication-time assignment
  alternative stays a map candidate, and nothing here reopens it.
- **Seeking the wrong goal:** judged on complete collision evidence, not
  fixture or receipt counts.

Limits:

- This verification neither integrates, commits nor publishes, and it runs
  no live external-source test.
- Stdout loss and process crashes after `applyReleasePreparation` stay
  outside D019's claim and are not judged here.
- D010, D012 and D013 remain boarded as before.
- A fresh independent verification must pass F2 after repair.
