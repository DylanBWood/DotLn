## Earlier findings re-checked

**F1 (FINAL-001) — fixed.** FINAL-001's rule has four parts:
- recovery never signals a process outside a recorded tree rooted at a recorded root identity whose parent is the recorded owner;
- a record with `browserPid` set and no recorded root is refused, or signals nothing;
- during the run, `observeOwned` does not adopt the current holder of `browserPid` before the root's identity is recorded;
- two regressions are required.

- **The repair.**
  - **Recovery guard.** `recover()` now requires a root whenever `browserPid` is set or the list is non-empty (`processes.ts:124-127`). It still requires a closed tree under that root, with the root's recorded parent equal to the owner.
  - **`observeOwned` seeding.** It returns without adopting unless a recorded root exists, its recorded parent is the owner, and its identity still matches the table (`processes.ts:38-43`).
  - **Root capture.** `runScenario` captures the root once, right after launch, requiring `parentPid === owner.pid` (`index.ts:345-351`). If the root is missing, it throws before the first `recordNow()` or the 250 ms recorder. The outer `catch` makes the run `unavailable`, and the `finally` closes the browser server. The only record it can save with `browserPid` set and an empty list is therefore `closed: true`, which recovery ignores.
- **`f1-recheck.mjs` (26/26 checks).** Every case used a `/bin/sleep 300` bystander, a child of the probe and not of any recorded owner:
  - **A, FINAL-001's exact shape.** The record was `{browserPid: <sleep pid>, processes: [], closed: false}`, with an owner identity that matches no process. Recovery threw `Invalid browser ownership record`. The record's bytes were unchanged, and the bystander stayed alive with no signal.
  - **B.** The same record shape with a real, exited owner identity was refused, and the bystander stayed alive.
  - **C.** A non-empty list without an entry at `browserPid` was refused.
  - **D.** A root at the bystander's pid whose recorded parent is not the owner was refused.
  - **E, a stale root.** The record named a root with the owner as parent and the bystander's pid, but a different start time. Recovery returned `[]`, adopted nothing, and the bystander stayed alive.
  - **F, `observeOwned` during a run.** A bare pid adopted nothing. A root with a foreign parent adopted nothing more.
  - **G, the positive kill path.** A Node owner spawned a detached `/bin/sleep` whose observed parent was the owner. Recovery refused while the owner lived. After the owner's SIGKILL, the child survived, and recovery signalled exactly `[29658]` (the child). The child was gone, the record was `closed`, a second recovery signalled nothing, and the bystander stayed alive.
  - **H, timezone.** Raw `ps -o lstart=` differs between `TZ=UTC` and `TZ=America/New_York` for one pid. `processTable()` returns the same UTC start time under a caller `TZ` of `America/New_York` and of `Asia/Tokyo`.
- **The executor's regressions.** Both re-ran and passed in my standalone run and in my gate:
  - `bare-pid-bystander`: both record shapes were refused, nothing was adopted, and the bystander (pid 32230) stayed alive and unsignalled. Its sentinel now sits at `browserPid`, where a faulty recovery would reach it.
  - `surviving-owned-tree-recovery`: the root and child survived the owner's SIGKILL. `recoverySignalledPids` was `[32294, 32293]`, the after-set was empty, the renamed live owner was still refused, and the fresh scenario was `available`.
