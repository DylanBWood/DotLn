It fails closed without data loss, but the order's removal of the retained stashes does not happen. **Expected:** `--apply` removes the listed published-order stashes after writing their inventory. ## Judgment and limits

The intended outcome is that the next order meeting a WO-111 defect runs a command instead of improvising. Items 1–6 and 9 achieve that, with the regressions noted. F1 leaves observed gap 7 open on the repository it names. F2 removes the foreign-writer advisory that the criterion keeps, most sharply on the explicit-observe path the Codex executor used.

Weighed against the traps:
- **Rule beating and drift to low performance:** these would follow from passing on green fixtures that never reach a packed ref or an in-window foreign write.
- **Policy resistance:** the repair must keep both the conservative packed-ref refusal and the own-write convenience without trading one for the other.
- **Escalation and tragedy of the commons:** these favor four bounded regressions and one repair pass over a redesign.
- **Success to the successful:** this does not make the loose-ref fixtures authoritative for the real stash stack.
- **Shifting the burden to the intervenor and seeking the wrong goal:** both would leave the operator discovering unprunable stashes or unread foreign bytes later.

Under Naive Interventionism, the repair should preserve the sound reflog rewrite, the ordering, and the separate gates. NoOp retains the inoperable prune and the silent credit, so it does not meet the order's objective.

Limits: the observations marked reading or suspected were not executed. The disposable copies share installed external dependencies. The session's own hooks come from the observer under judgment. A fresh VER must judge any repair.
