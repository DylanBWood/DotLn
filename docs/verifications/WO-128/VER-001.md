## Findings

### F1 — a skipped group member breaks the critical path before the gate is recorded (non-blocking, low)

- **Observed:** `scheduleSuites` adds the latest same-group result as a predecessor even when that task was skipped for a failed dependency (`scripts/test-runner.mjs:435–442`). The timeline keeps only started tasks (`:769`), so `gateCriticalPath` throws `Missing timeline predecessor` (`:806`) before `recordGateChecks` (`:841`). The gate would exit without recording its failed suites.
- **Expected:** a failed dependency produces a recorded failed gate, and a skipped task is never a timeline edge.
- **Reproduction:** an in-memory `scheduleSuites` table: `build`; `pre`, which fails; `c` and `b` in group `g`; `a` in `g` after `pre`; and `d`. It gives `b` the predecessors `build`, `pre` and `a`, and `gateCriticalPath` over the started rows throws for `a`.
- **Reach:** the only current group, `package-tests`, gives its members identical `after` lists, so they are skipped together and no member starts afterwards. The crash needs a future group with mixed dependencies.

### F2 — removing exclusivity adds about 204 s per fresh gate, and the evidence calls it a benefit (non-blocking, medium; operator decision)

- **Criterion:** AC4 is met as written. The order's Cost line promised that removal would save up to 206 s of concurrency-one time per fresh gate, and 07 asks verification to compare outcomes with that promise.
- **Observed, same source and sandbox, back to back:**
  - Shipped shared configuration (recorded gate, 23:10:16Z): 666.5 s wall-clock, 2,573 s of task time.
  - Both hook suites marked `exclusive` in memory (unrecorded): 462.3 s, 1,201 s of task time, all 78 tasks passing.
  - Builds took 2.3 s in each, so about 203 s of the difference comes after the build.
  - Per task, exclusive against shared: `harness-fixtures` 125.7 against 452.0 s; `process-debt` 69.6 against 212.7 s; `plan-refutation:fixtures` 177.3 against 501.9 s; `worktree` 151.0 against 306.1 s; `skeleton` 87.3 against 197.2 s; `console` 46.1 against 90.4 s.
- **Consistent with the order's own data:** the prior exclusive gate took 476.3 s with 1,229 s of task time, and the five shared runs took 689.5–699.4 s with 2,674–2,711 s. In that exclusive gate the two suites ran at 1.01× and 0.99× their standalone durations; in series 002 they averaged 3.75× and 3.44×. The standalone baselines were themselves taken with successful-call recording on, so recording overhead does not explain the difference.
- **Unsupported claims:** the README's "measured benefit … removal of required exclusive scheduling", and D008's rejection of retention as "inertia". The README and diagnosis say the changed-source comparison does not isolate scheduling cost; this comparison isolates it. [WO-126-D012](../../evidence/WO-126/decisions.md#wo-126-d012)'s reopening condition, isolation costs outweighing their benefit, is not met: the measurement points the other way.
- **Why non-blocking:** AC4 prescribes removal after five passes, so restoring the flags needs an operator amendment or a follow-up. For that decision: the exclusive run passed here (one sample, not five), and neither reliability repair, the collector bound or the signal fixture, depends on the shared class.

## Goal alignment

WO-128 aims to stop fresh gates failing on load. That rescue loop was the largest recorded waste on the route to independently verified work, and the deadline half removes it: the fixed collector bound is consistent with half of the twelve failures, and the series shows one command exceeding it.

- **Outcome against promise:** first-pass reliability was promised and observed, in five outside-sandbox runs and one sandboxed run. The time saving promised for removing exclusivity did not occur; the same-source comparison shows about 204 s added per fresh gate (F2).
- **NoOp and the alternative verdict:** failing the order would send a repair that AC4's text rules out. Passing with F2 puts the decision with AC4's author. Reopen this verdict if the final gate on these bytes fails, or if a recorded exclusive gate on this source does not reproduce F2's difference.
- **Naive Interventionism:** the observers keep every original bound except the collector's, keep collection read-only and add diagnostics without retries. Restoring exclusivity is a two-row change; the isolated class still exists.
- **System traps:**
  - Rule beating and seeking the wrong goal: AC4's pass/fail rule was satisfied while its purpose, regained parallelism, went the other way. F2 records that instead of treating five passes as the goal.
  - Tragedy of the commons: shared scheduling roughly doubles the compute each fresh gate consumes.
  - Success to the successful: the workaround was re-measured rather than kept for its existing investment, as the order asked; the measurement now favors it.
  - Policy resistance, escalation and drift to low performance: no retry, extra gate or raised constant was added, and the fixed bounds keep their values.
  - Shifting the burden: first-pass success removes the operator's rescue loop; F2 asks for one decision rather than a repair cycle.
