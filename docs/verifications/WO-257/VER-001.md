**F1 — accepted table vocabulary is lost at the merge.** The first batch returns
`Item | Location in the subject | Disposition`; the second returns
`Item | Where | Status`. Both pass `itemsTable`, including the `reviewed`
disposition and a subject location. `alignRow` matches literal header names
instead of the parser's semantic columns, leaving the second row's location and
disposition empty. The merged report is refused despite every worker having
returned an independently valid report. Expected: both rows survive with their
meaning and the round is filed. Repair rule: use one canonical column mapping
for validation and merging, preserving accepted vocabulary and reordered
columns; exercise both transport and recorded-part merges. The same alignment
helper also serves the consistency table, so its consumers need rechecking.

**F2 — the length fallback is not collision-free.** One batch returns two
schema-valid IDs: a 52-character ID and `F1`. The first becomes `b1.F1` through
the ordinal fallback, while the second also becomes `b1.F1` through the ordinary
prefix rule. The merger writes this duplicate pair without validating the final
result. Expected: distinct findings remain distinguishable, satisfy the result
schema, and can both be named by a later recheck. Repair rule: allocate unique
merged IDs across the whole result, retain the later kind/round prefix's length
budget, and validate the merged result before filing it. Recheck the transport
and recorded-part routes. This attacks the interaction left after round 3 held
the earlier ID-length repair.

**F3 — low-severity shared-input limit, boarded.** Two 198,980-byte captures are

The helper asks for shorter captures before it can partition documents. This is
