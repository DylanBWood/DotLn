## Observations — not defects, for the final reviewer and the planner

1. **The launch's crash evidence is larger and more specific than the doc
   records, and it sharpens the `ambiguous` label rather than contradicting
   it.** Alongside the one main-process report at 03:00:32, DiagnosticReports
   holds eighteen helper reports from 03:00:28 to 03:00:32 — nine
   `LM Studio Helper (GPU)` and nine `LM Studio Helper`, every one
   `EXC_CRASH`/`SIGABRT`/`Abort trap: 6` at `__pthread_kill` with
   `parentProc LM Studio`. The planner's 02:08 and 02:09 crashes have no helper
   reports and a different top frame (`v8::Module::GetIdentityHash`, the same
   in both, versus `ares_dns_rr_get_ttl` in the new one; the doc's "different
   top frames" is true of new-versus-old, not of the two old ones). A helper
   abort loop under a nested seatbelt profile is what a runner whose helpers
   apply their own sandbox would do if nested application were refused — and my
   own sandbox refused nested `sandbox-exec` outright
   (`sandbox_apply: Operation not permitted`). None of this proves causality;
   it does mean the next experiment should launch under the outer boundary
   alone, or under the corrected profile, before anyone concludes that the
   runner "crashes on `--run-as-service`". The decision packet's cadence bullet
   names a runner install or upgrade as its triggers; the cheapest trigger —
   one relaunch under a loopback-permitting boundary with a fresh one-launch
   authority — appears only in the capability row's next-experiment cell.
2. **Runner residue is wider than the one PID lock the doc names.** Files under
   `~/.lmstudio` modified between 02:55 and 03:05 that day (names only, models
   excluded): `bin/lms` itself (rewritten at 03:00:29, same embedded commit),
   `settings.json`, and thirteen `.internal/` state files including the PID
   lock. The launch was not side-effect-free on the runner's application data;
   contents were not compared because no pre-launch digest exists. A future run
   should hash that root, models excluded, before and after, since the order's
   "no config mutation" clause cannot otherwise be shown to hold for what the
   runner does to itself on wake.
3. **The Claude Code Bash boundary in this session is not a
   deny-by-construction network boundary.** `curl https://1.1.1.1` returned
   HTTP 301 in 2.2 s with no sandbox violation and the nine vendor URLs fetched
   normally, while binding a loopback listener
   (`PermissionError: [Errno 1] Operation not permitted`) and nesting
   `sandbox-exec` were refused. I therefore could not reproduce the executor's
   loopback A/B, and the order's parenthetical naming the sandboxed Claude Bash
   boundary as a network-denying one was untrue for this configuration. Whoever
   runs the next experiment must verify their boundary's egress behavior
   first-hand, as the executor did, before a socket snapshot means anything.
4. **One conservative mislabel.** Item 2 says unified memory is `documented
locally` "by the established environment precedent", but `environment.md`
   publishes only `macOS 15 on arm64`; the chip family, core count, and 48 GB
   are disclosed on the authority of this order, which already states them, and
   the 48 GB value is `observed` from `system_profiler`. Only the adjective
   "unified" is documented locally, in the order itself. The label under-claims.
5. **The v3 digest is not reproducible from the printed clauses; the v1 digest
   is.** The doc prints "public-safe clauses" for both profiles. For v1 they
   are the whole file; for v3 the recorded SHA-256 covers something more.
   Nothing turns on it, but a future doc should either print the whole profile

   the `/api/v1/models` path. Not a violation; a reader of the ledger line about
