# Organization and flow — breadth addendum

Research date: 2026-09-07. These records retain distinct additions, alternatives
and leads beyond OF-01–OF-08. DotLn scenes are original hypotheses, not verified
repository gaps or adopted decisions. No new browsing or experiments were
performed for this preservation pass.

## Team Topologies: substantively inspected distinctions

Page references here are PDF pages in the authors' six-article
[2020 mini-book](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf),
not invented chapter references to the 2025 second edition.

### TT-01a — Application coupling

Identify an actor-facing task that requires understanding unrelated
responsibilities. Try a narrower context boundary before package changes.
Preserve this cause separately from the five below; the source does not reduce
them all to splitting a monolith. [Pages 3–4](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=3).

### TT-01b — Shared-data coupling

Change one consumer's representation and check whether unrelated consumers must
change. Test the others' compatibility claims, rather than assuming shared data
or separate data is intrinsically preferable. [Pages 3–4](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=3).

### TT-01c — Build coupling

Identify checks genuinely dependent on a change versus costs from mechanical
bundling. This investigates evidence dependencies; it grants no permission to
omit required verification. [Pages 3–4](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=3).

### TT-01d — Release coupling

Demonstrate independent completed work retaining valid evidence while another
member remains unfinished. Related outcomes need not imply synchronized
progression. Preserve existing independent-workflow decisions.
[Pages 3–4](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=3).

### TT-01e — Model coupling

Test two applications using local terminology while retaining the shared
contract. Do not require an exhaustive common vocabulary merely to participate.
[Pages 3–4](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=3).

### TT-01f — Uniform-policy coupling

Compare two legitimate tool/presentation profiles and establish the small
common contract they need. Shared constraints can coexist with local choices.
[Pages 3–4](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=3).

### TT-02 — Use co-change as evidence, not an architectural verdict

Compare repeatedly co-changing authoritative surfaces with ownership. Separate
generated edits and release bookkeeping from substantive clusters before
proposing boundaries. The inspected article discusses temporal coupling through
co-change history. [Pages 5–6](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=5).

### TT-03 — Observe behavior before moving its boundary

Retain an execution and its failures before splitting an application,
responsibility or transport; repeat afterward, including unexpected communication.
Check whether the split improves independence rather than merely structure.
[Boundary discussion](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=7).

### TT-04 — Compare domain, risk and performance boundaries

Keep alternative sketches for one problem and evidence that distinguishes them.
A domain-shaped split remains a hypothesis. These alternatives occur in the
inspected article. [Pages 7–8](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=7).

### TT-05 — Detect interaction-mode mismatch

Compare collaboration that never ends with a supposedly self-service interface
that constantly needs provider help. One may need service extraction; the other
may need a simpler interface. More coordination is not automatically the fix.
[Page 14](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=14).

### TT-06 — Replace request-and-wait with a consumable capability

Try an already-authorized discoverable capability for evidence retrieval,
environment preparation or handoff. Can the consumer proceed without contacting
the provider? Removing coordination need not remove the underlying dependency.
[Page 15](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=15).

### TT-07 — Remove procedural burden while retaining useful thought

The mini-book distinguishes intrinsic, extraneous and germane cognitive load.
Observe problem understanding, procedural reconstruction and learning/judgment
in a task. Remove reconstruction without hiding information needed for judgment.
These are observations, not invented telemetry or equivalents of model tokens.
[Pages 11–12](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=11).

### TT-08 — Compare expert substitution with removing the obstacle

Assign a specialist in one case; repair the confusing environment, handoff or
guidance in another. Does later work still depend on exceptional intervention?
Preserve both options; the enabler idea is more than naming a helper.
[Pages 42–43](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=42).

### TT-09 — Retain reasons a consumer avoids a platform

Where both routes are authorized, observe voluntary return, abandonment and
workarounds. A satisfaction score alone does not establish fitness. This adds
consumer feedback to OF-05's burden measurement.
[Authors' platform discussion](https://teamtopologies.squarespace.com/key-concepts-content/what-is-a-thinnest-viable-platform-tvp).

### TT-10 — Make interaction expectations discoverable

Preserve version/testing expectations and communication preferences alongside
ownership. Challenge a handoff with incompatible version, unclear response and
unavailable recipient. Consumers should discover these without repeated status
questions. [Pages 46–47](https://teamtopologies.com/s/Organization-Dynamics-with-Team-Topologies-Mini-book-MB80.pdf#page=46).

### TT-11 — Show the delivery relationships inside a platform

The authors discuss nested delivery structure. A deferred inspector could show
outcome-owning workstreams behind a shared service rather than one undifferentiated
platform actor. No recursive actor schema or mandatory hierarchy follows.
[Platform discussion](https://teamtopologies.squarespace.com/key-concepts-content/what-is-a-thinnest-viable-platform-tvp).

TT-12–TT-14 come from the authors' current site, not verified second-edition
passages. [Current concepts](https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts).

### TT-12 — Preserve direct domain feedback

Compare relayed instructions with an authorized source-linked product brief.
Check whether intervening summaries erase a decisive need. This does not grant
contact or access authority beyond the task.

### TT-13 — Try protected discovery capacity

Compare a bounded owner-selected investigation allowance with only immediate
delivery tasks. Count useful discoveries and displaced delivery, including a
no-yield case. This is a profile experiment, not a default scheduling policy.

### TT-14 — Adjust one ownership boundary at a time

When repeated coordination points to misplaced responsibility, propose one
bounded reassignment and inspect existing commitments. Preserve authority and
evidence obligations during the transition.

## Meadows: additional systems experiments

### MS-01 — Separate incident, pattern, structural hypothesis and assumption

The project's iceberg aid provides this distinction, not a directly inspected
book passage. A failure view can show all four levels while labeling the latter
two hypotheses. One incident cannot establish system causality.
[Educational resources](https://donellameadows.org/systems-thinking-resources/).

### MS-02 — Separate average change from variability

Give the workshop histories with similar averages but different bursts or
oscillations. Require the intervention to explain the difference; test diagnosis
of behavior over time, not only bottleneck location.
[Dancing, practice 1](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

### MS-03 — Preserve existing self-maintenance

Identify local adaptation/recovery before replacing a manual practice. Include
a degraded-environment trial. A cleaner ordinary path can remove the only useful
recovery route. [Dancing, practice 2](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

### MS-04 — Return consequences to their originating decision

Connect later repair/interruption to the earlier policy/planning choice, not
only the encountering worker. A durable workstream or policy owner may receive
feedback after its worker disappears. This refines OF-02.
[Dancing, practice 6](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

### MS-05 — Compare reserve as a resilience/cost choice

Test zero, modest and excessive reserve with interrupted capacity. Measure
continuity, waiting, stale work and cost bearers. Reserve differs from unfinished
work; lowest inventory is not automatically best.
[Draft pages 7–8](https://donellameadows.org/wp-content/userfiles/bathtubs101.pdf#page=7),
[buffers essay](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/).

### MS-06 — Distinguish stopping growth from clearing backlog

Display level and inflow/outflow separately when arrivals equal completions but
old work remains. A falling total must distinguish accepted completion from
cancellation/invalidation. [Draft pages 3–6](https://donellameadows.org/wp-content/userfiles/bathtubs101.pdf#page=3).

### MS-07 — Preserve reusable assets as well as producing new ones

Compare another explanation with preventing an existing decision/artifact from
becoming inaccessible, stale or disconnected. Measure later reuse. The source
motivates examining outflows; this knowledge application is original. Knowledge
is not conserved physical stock: any model needs explicit units/rules.
[Draft page 7](https://donellameadows.org/wp-content/userfiles/bathtubs101.pdf#page=7).

### MS-08 — Name the kind and direction of intervention

Separate number changes, information access, rules, goals and revisable
structure. Test a meaningful opposite-direction alternative. The source
qualifies its ordering and parameter exceptions; a higher position is not an
automatic priority score. [Intervention essay](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/).

### MS-09 — Slow changes when observation cannot be faster

Hold evaluation delay fixed while varying incoming correction/work rate.
Compare with more frequent reaction. This preserves an alternative omitted
from OF-06's delayed-feedback discussion.
[Delays](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/).

### MS-10 — Observe opportunity reinforcing apparent success

The essay discusses success-to-the-successful feedback. Give equally capable
patterns unequal early exposure; inspect whether accumulated evidence allocates
still more chances to the favored pattern. Preserve eligibility/selection
history, not just success count. Compare QUA-13 as related original application.
[Intervention essay](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/).

### MS-11 — Review the learning method itself

After a bounded trial, propose changing observation window, trigger or evaluation
method, not only the evaluated action. Preserve the old method and rerun a
distinguishing case. Feedback about feedback is not automatic authority change.
[Dancing, practice 7](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

### MS-12 — Detect erosion of goals

Repeated failure may tempt acceptance reduction or success redefinition. Record
an owner-authorized goal change separately from improvement under the original
goal. This differs from optimizing the wrong metric.
[Dancing, practice 14](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

### MS-13 — Observe consequences beyond first use

Evaluate a correction/interface through later modification and recovery as well
as its first success. Early speed may create later maintenance.
[Dancing, practice 10](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

### MS-14 — Check where the burden moved

Inspect future-operator, other-workstream and consumer costs despite local
improvement. Preserve the boundary of evaluation alongside aggregate outcomes.
[Dancing, practices 11–12](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

### MS-15 — Keep multiple conditionally useful profiles

Retain patterns succeeding under different conditions; include a scene where
the previous loser becomes preferable. Contextual fitness/diversity differs
from competing explanations of one historical event.
[Dancing, practices 3 and 13](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).

## Goldratt: additional delivery and learning experiments

### GL-01 — Change only rules conflicting with the constraint decision

Name the existing rules that impede a proposed constraint intervention and
preserve unrelated rules. Subordination does not authorize a wholesale rewrite.
[Focusing steps](https://www.goldrattresearchlabs.com/introduction-to-toc).

### GL-02 — Retire yesterday's constraint policy

After the constraint moves, test an old restriction that now reduces completion.
Reassess its continued need, not merely the new bottleneck's identity.
[Step 5](https://www.goldrattresearchlabs.com/introduction-to-toc).

### GL-03 — Diagnose incompatible demands before blame

Goldratt's letter offers conflict as an explanation for undesirable behavior.
Inspect inconsistent objectives/incentives/instructions and test removing the
conflict before adding a warning. Sweeping human claims remain an author stance,
not proof that every actor/input/tool is trustworthy.
[Letter](https://www.toc-goldratt.com/index.php?cont=796).

### GL-04 — Test prediction instead of agreement with an explanation

The letter describes a gap between verbal assent and application. Let an
operator predict a scene before running it and retest changed conditions. This
teaching feature differs from model debate and implementation fixtures. The
actual P&Q exercise was not inspected sufficiently to reproduce it.
[Letter](https://www.toc-goldratt.com/index.php?cont=796).

### GL-05 — Make transition to everyday use credible

Preserve how a consumer tries, understands, recovers from and comes to rely on
a mechanism. Test that transition with the supplied artifacts. Technical efficacy
alone may not resolve adoption uncertainty; resistance is not assumed irrational.
[Letter](https://www.toc-goldratt.com/index.php?cont=796).

### GL-06 — Build a verified implementation-case bank

The letter proposes checked references. A DotLn case could link scene,
conditions, result, limits and reproducible evidence, retaining failed and
inconclusive cases. Use public/sanitized evidence and honest missing measures;
do not import requirements for private organization identities or business
figures. [Letter](https://www.toc-goldratt.com/index.php?cont=796).

### GL-07 — Keep ongoing improvement above finite work-order completion

The publisher describes ongoing improvement. A portfolio can continue improving
while each work order has finite acceptance/closeout. Do not let the philosophy
make work perpetually unfinished.
[Publisher](https://northriverpress.com/the-goal-30th-anniversary-edition/).

## Phoenix Project: additional operational experiments

### PX-01 — Supply quality capabilities while work is being shaped

Expose relevant requirements and checks earlier; compare with first discovering
the defect at final inspection. Preserve independent verification: prevention
and later acceptance answer different questions.
[Kim interview, pages 3–7](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/4876/2013_016_100_58540.pdf).

### PX-02 — Signal a scoped deviation where it occurs

The interview discusses Andon. Let an equipped actor expose a concrete deviation,
evidence and affected scope; test containment and authorized independent
continuation. A manufacturing line-stop analogy cannot freeze unrelated
workflows. [Interview page 7](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/4876/2013_016_100_58540.pdf#page=7).

### PX-03 — Practice recovery separately from testing a hypothesis

Repeat restart, unavailable-result or interrupted-handoff exercises. Measure
trusted-state restoration, retained evidence and operator effort. Identify the
durable improved artifact if claiming learning across fresh workers; repeated
calls do not establish weight changes. The indexed anniversary passage includes
practice/learning from failure; direct PDF access was limited.
[Indexed excerpt, printed page 91](https://itrevolution.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/TPP5_excerpt.pdf).

### PX-04 — Distinguish prepared capability from user activation

The interview separates deployment and release. Prepare a mechanism, prove its
dormant state, then exercise explicit activation. Record technical readiness
separately from making it available and observing user outcomes.
[Interview pages 5–6](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/4876/2013_016_100_58540.pdf#page=5).

### PX-05 — Experiment with delivery batch size

Compare one large change and observable increments, including total verification,
integration and recovery cost. This is an experiment, not a new synchronization
rule. It can inform recurring concept batches without making one size universal.
[Interview pages 5–6](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/4876/2013_016_100_58540.pdf#page=5).

### PX-06 — Detect escalating commitments after failure

Challenge a planner with delay/missed outcome. Inspect unsupported larger
promises, reduced quality work or expanded scope. A useful response revises the
plan within evidence and authority, preserving this part of the downward spiral
separately from the rework loop.
[Interview pages 2–3](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/4876/2013_016_100_58540.pdf#page=2).

### PX-07 — Compare producer and consumer views of one episode

Kim describes narrative supporting shared understanding. Show two short
evidence-linked accounts: what the producer treated as finished and what the
consumer still cannot do. Test handoff-defect recognition. Interpretations stay
separate from events; do not fabricate actor psychology.
[Interview page 10](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/4876/2013_016_100_58540.pdf#page=10).

## Source integrity and preserved alternatives

Current author sites, the 2020 mini-book and 2025 edition are distinct sources.
The Meadows draft's footers/charts do not establish published-book chronology.
The archives extraction for Dancing With Systems contained an unrelated
commercial insertion; substantive attribution uses the
[alternate official excerpt](https://donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/).
Positive/negative feedback means reinforcing/balancing, not good/bad outcomes.

Dramatic performance/adoption/burnout figures were not independently validated
and imply no expected DotLn percentage. Goal/Phoenix agreement partly reflects
inheritance. Stock-flow diagrams, co-change clusters, plausible stories and
replay do not establish real-world causality. Observation, narrower intervention,
preserving a working practice and declining a mechanism remain valid outcomes;
preservation never requires activation or a new order.
