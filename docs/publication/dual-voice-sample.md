# Dual-voice sample — evidence before “done”

Source base revision: `f2a4b232e1868691398964433c7e373fca4b84bb`

This sample adapts sequence, vocabulary, and depth while holding its three
claims fixed: a completion statement is not proof; a material claim must be
traceable to reviewed sources and applicable evidence; and a compressed view
must expose the mechanics it leaves out. The sample explains specified rules. It
does not claim that every planned inspection surface is implemented.

## Everyday AI-user voice

When an AI helper says “done,” treat that as a request to check the work, not as
the check itself. Look for the strongest evidence the task can provide: for
example, a passing test, a visible result, or an independently reviewed
artifact. The summary should lead you back to the source and show what was left
out, so you can inspect the mechanics before trusting a consequential result.

## Software-engineer voice

A terminal success message is not completion evidence. Bind the claim to the
strongest applicable executable or review artifact, retain links to its reviewed
source, and preserve implementation status. Any sparse or narrative projection
is intentionally lossy; it must expose the underlying state, decision,
authority, and evidence mechanics needed to audit the result.
