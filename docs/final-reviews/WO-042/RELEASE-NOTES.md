## Release overview

DotLn `v0.16.0` makes a compiled build unable to grant itself authority. Before this release a linked support could choose its own precedence layer, `safety-invariants` included, and an `allow` claim that won was written directly into the emitted `AuthorityEnvelope` and the WorkOrder operation lists. A build could therefore hand itself an effect its own active mechanic denied, and its tooltip could keep printing an authored reassurance such as "Deletion remains operator-owned" beside an envelope that allowed deletion. Since `v0.15.0` lowered that envelope into this repository's live `PreToolUse` hook, the gap reached real permission decisions rather than staying on paper. Compiler `0.8.0` closes it with three connected rules. A linked support may only narrow: a `deny` is legal at every one of the nine precedence layers, an `allow` is accepted only to restore an allowance the active mechanic already holds, and a `permission-guard` emission obeys the same floor for what it contributes. Widening now requires an explicit, exact `AuthorityGrant` that carries its own provenance and that a host-owned registry outside the submitted graph independently admits. And the tooltip's GRANTS and RESTRICTIONS are projected from the effective envelope, with authored prose moved under a section the render marks non-enforcing, so what a build shows and what its lowered hooks enforce can no longer diverge.

The same release carries an operator-directed expansion of how this repository's own executor works. Five atomic supports — Adjacent Repair, Decision Receipts, Follow-up Queue, Intent to Act and Operator Check-In — are equipped on the Contributor build, each independently switchable, none of them granting an effect or adding a hook. Adjacent Repair changes a standing rule: an encountered bug is no longer deferred merely because it predates the assignment. Decision Receipts is the separate support that records the evidence, the choice, the rejected options and a deferral's reversal condition, so preferring repair and producing paperwork stay two decisions rather than one.

## Read before upgrading

- **Some previously valid graphs now reject.** A linked support that allowed an effect outside its active mechanic's base allowances, or that added one through a `permission-guard` emission, compiled before and fails now with `AUTHORITY WIDENING`. The diagnostic names the support, the claim or emission id, the effect and the base list it violated, and offers two routes: unequip the support, or declare a host-admitted grant and remove the widening claim. No committed loadout used that path, so nothing in this repository needed migrating. This narrowing happens under an unchanged compiled-program contract `"1"`, which is an explicit compatibility decision recorded in product 10 rather than an accident.
- **Authority beyond the active mechanic now needs two independent statements.** A graph's `authorityGrants` entry is a request, not a permission. It is admitted only when the compilation environment's separate registry holds an entry matching its `grantId`, `version`, `grantedBy`, `effects`, `operations` and `repo`; otherwise the graph rejects with `AUTHORITY GRANT UNADMITTED`. `grantedBy` is exactly `operator`, `host-policy` or `registered-repository`; effects and operations are exact ids with no wildcards; `repo` must equal the compilation environment's repository, so a grant compiled into another repository rejects. The committed registry `packages/skeleton/loadouts/grants.json` ships empty, and `docs/control/local/authority-grants.json` is ignored and admits only `host-policy`. Adding an entry to the committed registry is an authority change and should be reviewed as one. The registry hash recorded in the trace and the harness manifest is an equality receipt, not authentication, and a grant's `reason` is explanatory text that admission does not check.
- **The compiled tooltip changed shape.** GRANTS and RESTRICTIONS are now the effective envelope's allowed and denied effects, with a granted effect annotated by its grant id and provenance. Authored `inspection.grants` and `inspection.restrictions` still appear, verbatim and byte-identical, under `AUTHORED NOTES (non-enforcing)`. Anything that scraped the old GRANTS section for authored prose should read the authored subsection instead. An authored note that names an exact opposite-envelope effect id now rejects the compile with `INSPECTION CONTRADICTION`; the token-boundary rule, including its Unicode punctuation behavior and its treatment of dotted and colon-qualified extensions, is stated in product 04.
- **An exact grant cannot punch a hole in a wildcard denial.** If a granted effect falls under a broader denied pattern, the compile rejects with `SEMANTICS UNSUPPORTED` rather than removing the wildcard, because removing it would widen other authority that the unchanged kernel cannot express an exception to.
- **The executor role's procedure changed.** Sessions resolving `resume: next` or `resume: fix` in this repository now receive five additional instruction fragments, and the former "Repair only those obligations" line becomes "Preserve those obligations and apply the equipped Adjacent Repair support to encountered defects." The independent verification, review, planning and release-close roles are unchanged, as is the read-only `status` and `times` behavior. `npm run adjacent -- list` and `npm run adjacent -- apply --file <request.json>` operate a worktree-local, ignored follow-up queue; its chat and inbox observations are actor-attested and it grants no permission to execute anything.
- **The planning gate no longer refuses a worktree for carrying required execution evidence.** The latest refutation receipt stays bound to its original committed subject, and a separate continuation comparison judges both HEAD and the workspace against it. It admits only an assigned release placeholder, an appended `## Execution record` section, and appended dated reassessments of existing capability ids, and it reports those separately from the judged subject. A genuine plan edit — objective, criteria, dependencies, non-goals, sequence, vision, roles or an existing capability row — still demands a matching refutation, and a clean-looking workspace cannot hide drift already committed to HEAD.
- **The feedback edition and the actor board move to WO-042 revision 002.** `npm test` and `npm run evidence:feedback` select `--edition WO-042 --revision 002`, and `SELF_HOST_EDITION` in `packages/console/src/collect.ts` follows. `feedback-evidence.mjs` gains `--revision NNN` for numbered subeditions; historical default paths are unchanged and earlier streams remain intact bytes. The console regression now requires both default self-hosted streams to replay and project, which is the bug this release fixes: the previous default named a WO-039 verifier stream that compiler `0.8.0` refuses to replay.
- **The generated bundle was regenerated and its build identity changed.** The saved Contributor graph still hashes `fnv1a64:06245f5c581212f1`; the installed build, which equips the five supports, is `fnv1a64:709272ae4905aaa6`. Hand-editing any generated surface still fails `npm test` on drift; change the loadout or a feedback unit and run `npm run harness -- emit`.

## Substantive changes

- **The monotone floor (`packages/compiler/src/authority.ts`, new).** `authorityDiagnostics` walks every link and checks each `authority.*` allow claim and each `permission-guard` emission entry against the active mechanic's base `authorityEnvelope.allowedEffects` and `workOrder.allowedOperations`, excluding their base denials, emitting `AUTHORITY WIDENING` with the offending id, the effect, the violated base list and both corrections. `applyAuthorityClaims` additionally drops any effect present in both merged base lists before claims run, so a denial in the union can no longer be outvoted by construction order. Precedence, commutativity, explicit pipelines and hard-conflict handling are untouched, and a support's `deny` remains legal at all nine layers, which is what a future Safety piece of the 5S set needs.
- **Grants, admission and provenance.** `AuthorityGrant` and the optional `LoadoutGraph.authorityGrants` collection are additive under schema version 1: absent normalizes to `[]`, entries sort by `grantId`, duplicate ids reject, and the code-DSL, function-table and statechart-JSON views all carry them and hash equal. Validation names the offending field for an unknown `grantedBy`, a wildcard or whitespace-bearing effect, a non-positive version, an empty or foreign `repo`, or a missing reason. `applyAuthorityGrants` runs after claims and is the only composition step that may add an allowed effect or operation or remove an exact base denial. Applied grants populate `CompiledProgram.grants` and `trace.authorityGrants = { applied, registryHash }`, and `lowerToHarness` carries both into the harness manifest as `grants` and `authorityGrantRegistryHash`. Grant-free programs omit all of it, which is what keeps every existing saved build byte-identical.
- **The host adapter (`scripts/lib/authority-grants.mjs`, new).** `readAuthorityGrantRegistry` reads the committed registry beside the source loadouts and the ignored local file, refuses a provenance class the file may not admit, and requires each to be a contained regular file. `compileRegisteredLoadout` supplies the result as the compilation environment's registry. Neither path is ever read from graph text, and the compiler itself performs no I/O.
- **Projected inspection (`packages/compiler/src/render.ts`).** `projectAuthorityInspection(program)` is pure and returns the effective allowed effects as GRANTS, annotating each granted effect with its grant id and provenance, and the effective denied effects as RESTRICTIONS. `renderCompiledDiff` prints those, then the authored strings under `AUTHORED NOTES (non-enforcing)`. `inspectionAuthorityDiagnostics` rejects an authored note that names an exact opposite-envelope effect id, matching the complete literal id against one Unicode boundary rule so that prose punctuation delimits it while dotted and colon-qualified extensions and prefixes do not. The actor board's Builds panel renders through the same compiler function, so its recorded fixtures were regenerated with the existing command and no console source changed for it.
- **Atomic executor supports (`packages/skeleton/src/loadouts/executor-supports.ts`, new).** Five default supports plus two alternative communication levels, all typed support facets using the existing `prompt-fragment` emission, with declared costs, no claims, no authority changes and mutual exclusion between the three communication levels. `contributorWithSupports` equips them on the Clean Room active, `contributorRolesFor` builds the executor procedure from the compiled component manifest and prompt fragments and refuses on projection drift, and the additive `role-procedure` harness adapter validates that an equipped support's compiled fragment is already present in the named role before recording its origin on that skill.
- **The follow-up queue (`scripts/lib/adjacent-queue.mjs` and `scripts/adjacent-work.mjs`, new).** An append-only, strictly validated JSONL fold in ignored worktree-local state. A candidate is admitted only with a recorded cause, concrete fix, contained relative paths, checks and priority; priority and scope are revisioned; a changed queue invalidates a previous check-in; starting an item requires its current announced intent and a fresh check-in; and mutation requires the selected order to be in an executor or fixer phase.
- **The planning-continuation gate (`scripts/lib/plan-continuation.mjs`, new).** `checkPlanContinuation` compares the receipt's judged subject with HEAD and with the workspace and classifies each difference, admitting only a release assignment that fills the exact activation placeholder with a semantic version, an appended execution-record section constrained in its headings and fields, and appended dated capability reassessments whose work order is in the reviewed sequence and whose capability ids already exist. Everything else raises. Historical receipts stay byte-identical and are never rewritten.
- **Documentation.** Product 02 gains §Authority grants and trusted admission and corrects the sentence that said a winning claim rewrites the envelope; 03 bounds step 3 of the composition system; 04 gains §Authority inspection projection with the boundary rule; 05 gains the executor-support, built-in modifier, intent-queue and orchestration/quality policy models; 06 records the release boundary; 07 replaces the discipline bullet under the operator's explicit supersession and restates the planning gate; 10 records the acceptance narrowing. The README release block, two dated capability reassessment rows, the publication index and both edition locks, the ledger with its rejected alternatives, and the planning map's now-allocated candidate follow.

## Progressive polish

- The two mutation survivors from the WO-108 campaign, `F-00001` (the tag half of link compatibility) and `F-00002` (equal-precedence rejection), are killed by two new negative tests. Both sites are inside the validation code this release rewrites, which is why the order authorized the cleanup here rather than nominating it. The historical WO-108 campaign records are unchanged.
- Existing compiler and skeleton fixtures that proved precedence by having a support grant itself `repo.delete` now start from an active mechanic that already allows it, so they still prove precedence rather than proving self-granting.
- Console expected JSON, terminal and HTML fixtures were regenerated through the existing `npm run evidence:console` command to pick up the new tooltip sections. No console rendering behavior changed.
