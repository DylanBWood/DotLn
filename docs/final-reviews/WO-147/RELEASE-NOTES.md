## Release overview

DotLn's resident no longer fails when the host lock it has just observed disappears before it can read it. Under live contention a holder can release `host.lock` in the microseconds between a contender's presence check and its stat or read; until this release that raised a raw `ENOENT` out of `WorkerStore.acquire`, and because `ResidentStore.transaction` retries only the live-host and recovery-busy refusals, a presence command or a resident transaction ended with an error that no event recorded and no operator could act on. Now the contender, which already holds the acquisition guard that excludes every new writer, inspects the lock once more and proceeds with what it finds: absence proceeds, and a present owner receives exactly the live, dead and shape judgments it received before. This release is for the operator who runs a resident beside daily work and for whoever takes the resident into its first unattended hour: it removes the one recorded ownerless way a contended store ends a resident transaction, and it does so without a new error class, a wider retry, a new lock or a new dependency.

## Read before upgrading

No event, envelope, schema, predicate, guard record or dependency changes, a store written by v0.32.1 opens unchanged, and the refusal surface is the same: a malformed or non-regular lock, an unreadable owner, a live host, a legacy ownerless guard and a missing or torn event log still refuse as they did. Three things are worth knowing. The proof is bounded to writers that honor the acquisition guard, so a same-user process that creates or removes `host.lock` behind the guard is outside it, and the single re-inspection is not a fixed point against one. The retirement `ENOTEMPTY` inference WO-143's verifier raised is closed as not reproduced in 20 deterministic attempts at the only pause point Node's native recursive removal leaves reachable, not disproved. And `ESRCH` remains the only establishment of a dead owner, so ambiguous liveness still refuses. The uncontended lock cycle is unchanged at about 17 to 19 ms median on the reference host. Skeleton moves to `0.34.1` and the console's exact pin follows it; the authority and feedback evidence editions are re-minted as WO-147 revision 001 with a fresh live self-host audit because the worker store is a registered source of both, and the console's self-host case is re-pinned to that edition.

## Substantive changes

**One guarded re-inspection.** `WorkerStore.acquire` factors its `host.lock` inspection into a local operation and calls it at most twice: once normally, and once more only when the first call fails with `ENOENT` from the lock's stat or read. Both calls run under the acquisition guard the process already holds, so a lock that vanished while the guard was held was released by its prior holder and nothing new can have arrived. A present lock on the second look is judged exactly as before. Any other failure rethrows, and `ResidentStore.transaction` keeps its retry pattern unchanged, so a missing log or a malformed lock still refuses. The two-process fixture pauses a real contender after it observes `host.lock`, has the real holder release, and asserts that both direct acquisition and a resident transaction complete with prior event bytes unchanged; with the re-inspection removed the fixture fails on raw `ENOENT`, which the executor and the verifier each reproduced.

**Filesystem codes survive the path-named decoder.** `StorePathError`, the error every path-addressed read wraps, now carries the underlying Node filesystem code, so the acquisition path can distinguish `ENOENT` while the lock's read stays inside the established decoder that WO-048's read-attribution invariant requires. Every other diagnostic keeps its prior wrapped message, and only the acquisition catch reads the code.

**The retirement probe.** A new fixture pauses a retiring owner before the recursive removal of its retired guard target, after `host.lock` is published, lets a delayed claimant hard-link a valid successor claim into that target, and resumes: in 20 of 20 attempts retirement completes, the original acquires and the delayed claimant is refused. `acquireGuard` is unchanged, and the decisions file records the probe and the reopening condition.

**Dead process ids taken at use.** Every WO-143 boundary fixture now spawns and waits for a short-lived child immediately before it needs a dead pid and asserts `ESRCH` before and after the boundary, instead of allocating one pid for a whole matrix. The named 344-boundary case that once observed a synthetic dead pid as live under WO-069's canonical gate passed ten sequential runs under the runner's concurrent load and ten alone on the final code, 6,880 crash boundaries with no live-pid observation, plus one full gate and one isolated run at verification and one more gate at final review.

**Capability row and product 03.** The capability table's dated `runtime.resident` reassessment now records the judged level: 2, dependable within the tested local process-crash scope, which now includes live contention between conforming local writers, with the contention limit WO-143 wrote in removed and what remains stated. Product 03 §Operator-presence policy states the bounded re-inspection and that pid reuse in the fixtures is observed rather than assumed.
