# WO-233

DotLn now cleans up after itself. Every temporary root the lifecycle, a gate or an executable fixture creates goes through one helper that records its owner, class and retention, and the places the lifecycle already runs do the removing: a gate removes the roots its passing suites created after it records its row and keeps a failed suite's roots for the diagnosis window; the Claude Stop hook and the Codex lifecycle completion remove only the completing session's own scratch and dead worker captures once its dispatch's completion is recorded; release close prunes after its worktree cleanup and records what it removed, retained and could not; an integration drops its own stash when its application completes; and `harness prune` keeps its spelling for the operator, now judging `temporary-root`, `session-scratch` and legacy `dotln-*` candidates under the host lanes lock, making read-only leftovers writable, treating a removed repository as observed absence and refusing a running gate by name instead of racing it. The launchpad export suite installs once per manifest-and-lockfile key into a warm fixture the gate retains; the gate row carries the files created and deleted, the bytes written and the `fseventsd` resident bytes; and the kit carries the retention keys and their template, so an updated instance prunes its own residue, which the instance-history suite proves with a synthetic integrate and close. Under the operator's amendment of 2026-10-27, `worktree integrate` is admitted for an active order behind `main` and runs from the main checkout's current code against the registered worktree, so a stale worktree takes the current gate and its handoff row is the one final review reuses. Under the operator's scope expansion of the same day, every execution and fix takes three fresh independent reviewers, the third checking write-backs, dependencies, versions and fixture metadata.

Why it matters: WO-192 measured 85 integration stashes (41.30 GB), 641 session scratch trees (9.7 GB) and, at this order's filing, 24,526 `dotln-*` roots (14 GB) that the operator deleted by hand over about two hours, with the cleanup commands handed to them mid-turn and a prune refusal that stopped their chain; every complete review also paid an `npm ci` per export fixture after the first. Nothing the lifecycle creates now outlives its use without a recorded owner and a retention class, and nothing is handed to the operator to delete ([the order](../../work-orders/WO-233-dotln-cleans-up-after-itself.md), [the filing pass](../../planning/wo192-fixes-and-example-drift-2026-10-26.md), [D004](../../evidence/WO-233/decisions.md#wo-233-d004--cleanup-follows-ownership-and-serializes-with-gates), [D013](../../evidence/WO-233/decisions.md#wo-233-d013--preserve-the-operators-cleanup-as-the-cause-of-the-baseline)).
