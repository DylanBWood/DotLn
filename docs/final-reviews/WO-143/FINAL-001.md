## Observations from reading the diff

**O1 — the polling body was not a no-op after a kill, and the order said it was.** WO-068 FINAL-001 O2 and this order's observed gap both say that once `killed` is set the transaction body "does nothing". It did one thing: `if (at >= nextSampleAt) tx.sample(at)` ran regardless of `killed`, so a drain that outlived an observation deadline appended `ClockSampled` events. With `if (killed) continue` those samples stop, which is what criterion 4 requires and what its fixture asserts ("expired deadlines must not cycle locks after kill"). Samples have a consumer: `resident-state.ts` folds human-idle expiry and the machine's `idle-expired` transition on each `ClockSampled`. Those folds are not lost, because the final transaction samples the clock before it records the outcome, but one that falls due during a drain is now folded when the actor exits instead of at its deadline. The delay is bounded by the drain, the time a killed actor takes to exit; an actor that never exits stalled the loop before this order and still does. I judge that acceptable and inside what criterion 4 asks for, and the release notes say the loop stopped sampling as well as locking so the log's changed shape is not a surprise. My first draft of this paragraph claimed no consumer existed; the search showed otherwise and the paragraph was rewritten before filing.

**O2 — every failure inside guard acquisition becomes the busy-or-interrupted refusal.** `acquireGuard` wraps its whole body in a bare `catch` that cleans up and throws `refuse()`. That is the order's rule, "every doubt refuses", and the released code did the same around its one `mkdirSync`. The catch is now wider: a full disk or a permission error while preparing the owner directory reads as a busy guard and is retried 200 times before the redacted line appears. Conservative and by design; noted because it is where an operator's first confusing refusal is likely to come from.

**O3 — a throw from `guard.release()` would leave the process holding its own lock.** It runs in `acquire`'s `finally` after `host.lock` is published and `#locked` is set. VER-001's unreproduced `ENOTEMPTY` inference is the one path I can construct to it, and it needs a third process linking into a directory in the microseconds between two unlinks inside another's native `rmSync`. The consequence would be an error from `acquire()` with the lock held until that process exits, after which the next start reclaims it. It is already carried in D004's follow-up; I add nothing to it except agreement that it belongs there.
