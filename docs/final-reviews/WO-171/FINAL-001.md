## Goal-aligned judgment

The order is not on a lifecycle critical path: the apply is an operator command outside a governed session. Its promised benefit is that the operator's residue command finishes. On 2026-09-27 that meant 5.9 GB of integration stashes that one apply would remove. It must do so without the prune undoing WO-170's recovery of 2,141 usage rows. The question for this review was whether the speedup dropped any guard the per-candidate re-plan provided, and whether the usage guard holds for every copy name the producer makes.

- **Policy resistance** is the material lens, in two directions. A faster apply could erase the usage copies WO-170 exists to recover. D004 and D013 answer that with a digest rule, and the safety helper confirmed it with real reconciliation output, including a copy suffixed on both components. In the other direction, the digest rule is stricter than WO-170 as filed can satisfy. WO-170 writes usage totals by role and nothing else, so under D004 no lane is ever released after WO-170 closes. I recorded that as the open row `FUP-dc6c139003bd4b89` for the next planning pass, before WO-170 activates, and put it in the PR body.
- **Rule beating** decided how I checked. The fixtures count plans and publication calls rather than timing them. The fixture helper confirmed that the plan count is exactly one (zero also fails), and that the injectable is asked exactly once per order. It confirmed the fake `git` logs every `ls-remote`, because `runGit` always passes `-C`. The one place a rule can be satisfied without its intent is the usage digest found anywhere in the snapshot's text (D014).
- **Shifting the burden:** the operator no longer faces an apply estimated at about three hours; the last recorded one ended with exit 143. A stopped apply now resumes by running again. A stash drop stopped inside its locks still needs the operator (D007, boarded).
- **Tragedy of the commons:** the listing keeps 73 lanes (45.6 MB) for usage until a snapshot names their copies. The 5.85 GB of integration stashes are unaffected and become removable in one apply.
- **Drift to low performance:** VER-001's failure and the repair are recorded as they happened (D012, D013). This review's own listing took 17,996 ms against the executor's 16,400 ms, and both figures are given.
- **Escalation, success to the successful, seeking the wrong goal:** no gate, event, grant or eligibility rule is added. Runtime is not the sole success measure: the usage retention and the refusal on a changed candidate are part of the result.
- **Naive Interventionism:** I did not change verified source for the digest match. No producer writes a digest into `meta.json`, and the field belongs to WO-170. D014 boards it with a reopening condition.
- **NoOp** leaves the apply at an estimated three hours of per-candidate GitHub calls. In practice that means the stashes stay on disk, and a working apply without the usage reason would delete the rows WO-170 recovers.

It is a few kilobytes in an ignored lane that is never listed or inventoried. A `.partial` that is a directory refuses every run with EISDIR, which is the safe side. D014's follow-up carries it.
