# Fresh full gates pass first time: every gate deadline follows the declared load and a hit names its peers

Twelve fresh full gates failed on 2026-09-11/12 on load-sensitive deadlines, and each one needed a rerun. On this source, five consecutive `npm run test:full -- --fresh` runs on the operator's host passed first time with all 78 tasks executed and none reused, and the verifier's sandboxed fresh gate passed too. The runner now declares each task's load class (shared under a cap of four, or isolated) and passes a load factor of twice the slot count to its children; a bound that exists to catch a hung process is derived from the suite's measured baseline through that factor, and every other reachable finite bound is inventoried and protected by the peer cap. A hit records the site, the bound, the measured duration, the tasks running beside it, the host load and a classification, in a retained diagnostic beside the gate row.

The console collector keeps its read-only command set; its per-command bound is the larger of the 60 s floor and the measured 17.2 s `release:list` baseline times the load factor, 137.6 s at four slots. In the accepted series one command took 61.75 s, which the old constant would have failed.

Each gate row records, per task, its start, end, the tasks running at its start, its predecessors, class and peer cap, plus an offline critical path; `node scripts/measure-gates.mjs --check` recomputes it from the stored row. The first accepted row's chain is 688.4 s of its 689.5 s wall-clock and runs through `plan-refutation:fixtures` (523.8 s) and the release cases, the entry evidence for product 07's cold-gate candidate.

The exclusive scheduling of `harness-fixtures` and `process-debt` was re-measured and removed, as criterion 4 prescribes after five shared passes. The verifier's same-source comparison shows that the removal costs about 204 s per fresh gate rather than saving the 206 s the order predicted: 462.3 s with the two suites exclusive against 666.5 s shared, consistent with the series (689.5–699.4 s over 2,674–2,711 s of task time) against the prior exclusive gate (476.3 s over 1,229 s). [D010](https://github.com/DylanBWood/DotLn/blob/main/docs/evidence/WO-128/decisions.md) records the cost and leaves the configuration to the operator; neither reliability repair depends on the shared class, and restoring it is two rows.

Two fixture defects found on the way are repaired at their cause: the planning cost fixtures declare their own empty budget acceptances instead of copying the live file, and the release-suite signal test starts the suite in its own process group and waits for an explicit readiness file before signalling it (series 001's fifth run failed there, and a quiet-host negative control showed shell-only signalling with a waiting child fails deterministically). The review repairs VER-001 F1: a task skipped for a failed dependency is no longer a scheduler predecessor, so a future group with mixed dependencies cannot crash the gate before it records its failed suites.

Application target `v0.17.3` (patch) above the local `v0.17.2`; skeleton `0.15.3` with regenerated runtime pins and snapshot; console `0.1.5`; compiler `0.9.1` and the kernel unchanged; no new dependency. The declared load policy is part of each suite's reuse key, so suite successes recorded before this change execute once more. Fresh WO-128 authority (001) and feedback (001) editions are selected in `docs/evidence/current.json`.

[VER-001](https://github.com/DylanBWood/DotLn/blob/main/docs/verifications/WO-128/VER-001.md) passed all six criteria with two non-blocking findings. [FINAL-001](https://github.com/DylanBWood/DotLn/blob/main/docs/final-reviews/WO-128/FINAL-001.md) records the review: the series checker on both series, the twelve gates re-read from the retained store, the fixtures, the F1 reproduction, a deadline sweep, byte identity between the build and the pinned snapshot, a clean-room screen and the canonical evidence gate on the review tree. Non-blocking: outside-sandbox execution is attested, not observed; the 2.0 per CPU load threshold is declared, not measured; each gate row carries the four intentional negative-test hits; diagnostic directories have no retention.
