# The critic of the critics: the brief the 2026-11-16 pass used

Record of the [2026-11-16 planning pass](the-kit-stops-no-work-2026-11-16.md).
This is the brief its research worker was given, with the session's own
paths taken out and the two sources that were particular to that day named
as fields. The worker's standing rules (read-only, no gate, nothing read
under the ignored local control lane or intake, no commit header or author
line) stood before it, as the kit puts them before every worker's brief.
[Its report](the-kit-stops-no-work-2026-11-16-critic-of-the-critic.md) is
kept beside this file. A later pass fills the fields and launches it
unchanged, until WO-279 makes it a kind the kit launches by name.

Fields: `@SINCE@` is the last planning pass before this one; `@RECORDS@`
are every criticisms file and every decisions file written to since then,
whether its order is closed, open or was never an order (a planning
pass's own critic and the corrections recorded in its document are among
them); `@OPEN_CRITIC_ORDER@` is the open order that builds or changes the
critic, if one is open. The pass of 2026-11-16 named the orders closed
since the last pass, in this field and in the wording of item 3, which
left out an unfinished order's records and a planning session's; a review
reader found that, and both are by interval from this file on.

```text
You are the critic of the critic: an independent review, read-only. Judge
the loop, not the working sessions.

Background. A critic of conduct runs beside a working session and writes
criticisms of how the session is working. Its points are in @RECORDS@. The
operator's standing complaint, in substance: the critic's findings do not
find their way back into making the roles and the process better, the same
failures recur, and nothing checks that a finding changed anything.

1. Inventory. Read every criticism written since @SINCE@. Group them into
   recurring failure kinds, as many as the evidence shows, each named in
   plain words. Keep the classes an earlier pass used, so that its
   dispositions can be traced. Give each kind its count and the points
   that belong to it.

2. Trace. For each kind, find what happened to it after it was written:
   (a) it was answered only inside that session, by a reply or a decision
   of the order; (b) standing text changed because of it: a role's text
   and the source it is generated from, the root instruction file, a
   product document, a seed, a brief; (c) a mechanism changed: a check, a
   hook, a command's default; (d) nothing. Cite a file and line or a
   commit for each claim.

3. Recurrence. For each kind, find whether it happened again later: in
   later criticisms, in the operator's recorded corrections in the
   decisions and pass documents written to since @SINCE@, or in the final
   reviews' findings. A kind that came back after a change was made for it is the
   most important thing to report: say what the change was and why it did
   not hold. Look first for what the kit itself said at the moment of
   choice: a command's output, an order's form, a record's schema, a
   sentence of role text that contradicts another.

4. The loop's structure. Say plainly where a criticism goes today, who is
   obliged to read it, what obliges a change to standing text or a
   mechanism, and what would show that a change worked. Read
   @OPEN_CRITIC_ORDER@ and say whether, as written, it closes the loop.
   Name what is missing.

5. The critic itself. From the operator's corrections, name what the
   operator had to catch by hand that a critic with this charter would not
   have caught, or did not catch, and what its charter or cadence would
   need in order to see it.

6. Recommendations. At most seven, ordered by how much of the operator's
   time each would have saved, each as: the failure kind; the change
   (prefer a changed default or a removed step to a new rule; prefer one
   sentence in the text a role reads at that moment to a paragraph
   elsewhere; never a new refusal or hard stop); where exactly it goes;
   and the observation that would show it worked.

Return: the table of failure kinds (kind, count, what changed, whether it
came back, with evidence), the answer to item 4 in one paragraph, and the
recommendations. State the boundary of what you read and mark anything
unverified. Never guess: write "unknown" with the source you tried. Be
direct; do not soften findings.
```
