## The chair (the planning session, Claude Fable 5.2)

Seat: the chair (the planning session, Claude Fable 5.2 at max). Written at 16:20 UTC on 2026-11-25, before any seat's round-1 paper was read. It is one input and carries no more weight than a seat's.

### The constraint

The operator, as he said at the founding. Concretely: a single core line that needs him at every phase boundary, every change to a judged criterion and every merge, and that carries one order at a time. Evidence: nothing moves in his four-day absence; 45 h of WO-288's first 145 h was idle between phases; a small order's working phases sum to about 5 to 6 h and its elapsed time to 8 to 10 h; 140 messages and 52 whole critic reports reached him in a week; every fix an instance waits on queues behind whatever order holds the line.

- Exploit: nothing reaches him that a session, a planner or a refuter can decide; an order that will not converge leaves the line at a fixed bound; the line carries what an instance needs before what core's own process wants.
- Subordinate: planning files only what the line can start within a week; reviewers and critics work to a budget and never hold a gate on a search; batch size is one small order.
- Elevate: `drive:` working in core so phases chain without him; parallel lanes where surfaces are disjoint; later, work that stacks to "awaiting merge" while he is away, by a policy he elects.

### Traps

- Shifting the burden, twice over: to him (authorizations, waivers, phrases) and to rules (ten recurring complaints, each answered with a rule, each back). Way out: fixes that are a removal, a default or a record's form.
- Seeking the wrong goal: 41 criteria as worded, and no finding anywhere in 46,000 lines, in place of what an instance can now do. WO-288's two failing things give an instance nothing.
- Escalation: verifier and executor in an arms race (one reader, then twenty, then ten every round; a reader of 2,930 lines grown to 4,147). Way out: narrow the contract so the input is invalid, never widen the code to answer an invented input.
- Rule beating: repairs that close the returned example and not its class; judges building inputs nobody writes; the planner's literal compliance today.
- Drift to low performance: the risk in my own general rule that only listed scenarios can return an order. Way out: keep the standard and change the work (small, closed, strict).
- Success to the successful: the one order that is running attracts every new part, which makes it less likely to ship.
- Tragedy of the commons: his attention, the shared files every order edits, the follow-up register, the role roots' bytes.

### Fragility

Fragile: large batches (verifications grow faster than size), one line, all-or-nothing acceptance, a tolerant reader, him as a single point. Remove: the tolerant reader; the per-round sweep; whole critic reports pushed to him; the queue form as a default; sentences that a record's form can replace. Did harm: the queue form, Part F added to a running order, the rule of 2026-11-16 that every round reads everything, the narrow direction of 2026-11-23, my amplification today. Could gain from stress: each failed verification should tighten a contract or add a test, and his absence should be a resource by a policy he sets.

### Decisions

- **D1.** Close WO-288 under a bounded direction, with one change to my draft: restore `main`'s reading of a `--format json` return and drop the clause of criterion 14 that asked for more. The widened reader is the one piece that both loops and can lose a finding silently; it is separable (20 names, about 794 lines, reached only from `readReturn`); restoring `main`'s function removes its regression class by construction. Criteria 25 and 27 are settled on named evidence, all tests re-run on the handed-over tree, the repair's diff is reviewed, and the order gets one verification and one final review, then parks. Cost: a few more hours of repair. Gives up: the convenience the clause wanted. Wrong if: the restore cannot be made without touching other Part B features.
- **D2.** Programs take facts from records under contract: JSON files, or a JSON block with exact marker lines inside a Markdown file. Prose is never read for a fact. The Markdown parser stays as the document check's instrument for links and form. One linter and schema per record kind, built on the dependency-free checker the console already has. Move three things, each a small order that deletes a reader: a review worker's result (strict JSON, no search); a work order's contract block (header facts and criteria as scenarios); a report's judgment block. Then stop, and reopen only on a failure traced to a prose read. This restores his founding design (the log is the truth, Markdown is a view) at the pace the line allows, and keeps the Markdown he likes to review.
- **D3.** A criterion is one behavior, its command and a finite list of scenarios. A claim over a set is over a list a step builds. Until the contract block makes a missing list a schema failure, the planner and the refuter judge closedness before an order starts.
- **D4.** Keep the standard: an unmet criterion or a reproduced regression on supported input blocks. Make "supported" decidable by strict contracts. After a repair: every test re-run on the exact tree, every earlier blocking finding reproduced, the repair's diff and what it reaches reviewed, and no fresh search over code the repair cannot reach. Full searches happen twice by design, at the first verification and at the final review. The third failed verification, or the second return of one criterion, sends the order to a re-cut by a planner worker and a refuter (cut the part, amend, or withdraw), with what passed kept; he reads the outcome and may reverse it. To his question: a change invalidates every earlier test result, and the kit re-runs them all; it does not invalidate an earlier reading of code the change cannot reach, and a repeated search never comes back empty on a large change.
- **D5.** Small orders by default. The economy he wanted from combining comes first from driving, which removes the idle between phases. A combined order only where the members share a design, planned from the end state, with each part verified by itself so one part cannot hold the others. A train (several orders verified alone, one release) is declined for now as machinery, to be reopened if a driven small order still costs too much.
- **D6.** After WO-288: WO-289 with WO-290; WO-294, moved up because driving exploits the constraint for every later order; WO-291 and WO-292; then the re-cut of WO-293, WO-295 and WO-296 with the strict review result; then the order contract block. Stop: the cadence critic's whole reports to him, until a finding of it has changed something; filing orders against an unmerged order's text or beyond a week of the line; answering a complaint that has already come back with a sentence.
- **D7.** A standing page of what he wants, read first by every pass, with his five tests as the measures a pass reports. A directive is answered in the same turn with its category, the narrowest mechanism, its cost, the do-nothing case and a recommendation, and enters force at a pass's merge with a reopening condition. A pass that adds a rule or a check names what it removes or the measured failure it prevents. A room like this one is for a fork in the architecture or an order that hits the bound, and the refuter is the standing instrument.
